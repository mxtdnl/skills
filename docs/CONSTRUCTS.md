# 5C Skill Definitions

**Status: DRAFT for sign-off.** Not yet approved. When approved, complete the sign-off block at the end. No measure or item may be written against a skill until its definition is approved.

These are working definitions for an assessment and development tool used with undergraduate students. They are not attributed to Hult and do not claim to reproduce any official Hult wording.

## How to read this document

Each skill has:

- a **working definition** (one sentence);
- **facets**: the parts of the skill the tool measures and develops separately;
- for each facet, **observable behaviours** of a student who is strong and of one who is still developing;
- **out of scope**: what the skill does *not* include in this tool, so that skills do not overlap and scores are not over-interpreted;
- **planned measures**, mapped to facets in the coverage table at the end.

Facet codes (e.g. `CO1`) are used in the item bank and data exports.

## 1. Collaboration

**Working definition.** Working with others towards a shared goal by sharing and seeking information, coordinating effort, and handling disagreement constructively.

### CO1. Information sharing and integration

Making sure the team has what it needs to decide well: offering what you know, asking for what others know, and combining the two.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| Shares information that others are unlikely to have, without being asked | Shares mainly what everyone already knows |
| Asks quieter members directly for their view or knowledge before a decision | Accepts the first proposal before everyone has contributed |

### CO2. Coordination and contribution

Organising the work so that it gets done: agreeing who does what, following through, and keeping the team on track.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| Proposes a clear division of tasks and deadlines, and checks progress against them | Waits for others to organise the work |
| Delivers their own share on time, or warns the team early when they cannot | Misses deadlines without warning, or silently does other people's work |

### CO3. Constructive disagreement and feedback

Raising problems and differences of view in a way that improves the team's work and keeps the team working together.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| Raises a concern about the team's direction early, with a reason, and commits to the decision once made | Avoids raising concerns, or keeps reopening decisions after they are made |
| Gives a teammate specific, respectful feedback on underperformance directly | Complains to others, or ignores the problem |

**Out of scope.** Leadership as a formal role; friendliness or popularity; general personality (e.g. agreeableness); the quality of the team's final product. Real collaborative behaviour in actual group work can only be fully evidenced by peer evaluation, which is planned for a later integration with Hult systems.

## 2. Curiosity

**Working definition.** The drive to notice gaps in one's own knowledge and to seek out new information, questions and perspectives, beyond what a task requires.

This definition follows the information-gap view of curiosity (Loewenstein, 1994): curiosity arises when a person notices that they know some, but not all, of something.

### CU1. Information seeking

Choosing to find out more, especially where one recognises a gap in one's own knowledge, even when it costs time or effort.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| Spends limited time or effort on finding out things they know they do not know | Seeks information only when required, or only on topics already familiar |
| Follows up a puzzling fact by looking for the explanation | Accepts a surprising fact without wanting to know why |

### CU2. Questioning

Asking questions that probe causes, explanations and implications rather than only surface facts.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| Asks "why", "how" and "what if" questions about a new situation | Asks mainly "what", "when" and "who" questions, or few questions at all |
| Generates questions that would change how the situation is understood if answered | Generates questions that only confirm details already given |

### CU3. Openness to the unfamiliar

Engaging willingly with unfamiliar subjects, methods and viewpoints, including ones that challenge one's own.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| Chooses to explore topics outside their field of study | Avoids topics outside their comfort zone |
| Seeks out views that differ from their own in order to understand them | Dismisses unfamiliar views without engaging with them |

**Out of scope.** General knowledge (knowing a lot is not the same as wanting to know more); intelligence; academic performance. *Boundary with Critical Thinking:* CU3 concerns the **willingness** to engage with challenging views. Whether a person **evaluates** them well belongs to Critical Thinking (CT2, CT3).

## 3. Critical Thinking

**Working definition.** Evaluating claims, evidence and reasoning, including one's own, in order to reach well-justified conclusions held with appropriate confidence.

### CT1. Logical reasoning

Drawing valid conclusions from given information and recognising what would and would not test a claim.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| Identifies the cases that could prove a rule wrong, not only the cases that fit it | Looks only for examples that confirm a rule |
| Distinguishes what follows necessarily from what merely seems likely | Treats plausible conclusions as if they were proven |

### CT2. Argument and evidence evaluation

Identifying assumptions, flaws and the strength of evidence in an argument.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| Spots an unstated assumption or a gap between evidence and conclusion | Accepts a conclusion because it sounds reasonable or matches their view |
| Judges what evidence would most strengthen or weaken a claim | Treats all evidence as equally strong, or relies on anecdote |

### CT3. Calibration and self-correction

Holding conclusions with confidence that matches the evidence, and revising them when new evidence arrives.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| Is more confident when right and less confident when wrong | Is equally confident whether right or wrong (usually overconfident) |
| Changes a view when presented with better evidence and can say why | Defends a view regardless of new evidence |

**Out of scope.** Subject knowledge in any particular discipline; mathematical skill beyond basic proportions; scepticism as a personality trait (doubting everything is not the same as evaluating well).

## 4. Creativity

**Working definition.** Generating ideas that are both new and useful for the purpose, and moving between different kinds of idea.

The "new and useful" definition follows the standard definition in creativity research (Runco & Jaeger, 2012). The facets CR1–CR3 follow the divergent-thinking tradition associated with Guilford.

### CR1. Fluency

Producing a large number of relevant ideas in response to an open problem.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| Keeps generating relevant ideas after the obvious ones are used up | Stops after the first few obvious ideas |

### CR2. Flexibility

Producing ideas of different kinds, from different categories or approaches.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| Moves between very different kinds of idea (e.g. from using an object as a tool to using it as a material) | Produces many variations of the same idea |

### CR3. Originality

Producing ideas that few others produce, while remaining workable.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| Offers uncommon ideas that still clearly work for the purpose | Offers mainly the ideas most people give first |
| Combines ideas from unrelated areas | Stays within the usual context of the problem |

### CR4. Problem finding

Spotting and framing problems, needs or opportunities that are not stated.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| Identifies problems in a situation that others overlook, including indirect consequences | Identifies only the problems that are stated or obvious |

**Out of scope.** Artistic or aesthetic skill; creative achievement (the tool measures creative *thinking*, not a record of creative output); selecting and refining the best idea (convergent judgement), which is a possible later addition. Divergent-thinking tasks measure part of creative *potential*, and results must be described that way.

## 5. Communication

**Working definition.** Conveying meaning in writing so that the intended reader understands it accurately and with little effort.

### CM1. Clarity and structure

Making the main point clear and easy to find, and making every reference unambiguous.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| States the main point first, then the supporting detail | Buries the main point after background or courtesies |
| Leaves no doubt about who or what each pronoun refers to | Writes sentences that a reader can interpret in two ways |

### CM2. Audience and register

Adapting words, tone and level of detail to the reader and the situation.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| Replaces jargon with plain words when the reader is not a specialist | Uses the same vocabulary for every reader |
| Uses a formal tone for a formal reader and a relaxed one where it fits | Mixes formal and casual tone in the same message |

### CM3. Explicit reasoning

Giving the reasons and links a reader needs to follow from facts to a conclusion.

| Strong | Developing |
|---|---|
| States why a decision follows from the facts given | Presents a conclusion with no stated reason, leaving the reader to guess |

**How the facets are measured.** Each facet is measured in two ways:

- **receptively**: recognising which of two versions is clearer, and diagnosing what makes a passage unclear;
- **productively**: rewriting a passage.

The receptive tasks carry most of the score, to limit the advantage of native English speakers (see out of scope).

**Out of scope.** Spoken presentation, listening and non-verbal communication (possible later additions); grammar and spelling accuracy as such; general English proficiency. Any written task in English will partly reflect English proficiency. The tool limits this by favouring selection tasks, never scoring spelling or grammar, and checking items for bias against students writing in a second language during the pilot.

## Questions for the reviewer

1. **Collaboration:** should inclusion (making sure every member is heard) be a separate facet, or stay within CO1 as drafted?
2. **Curiosity:** is CU3 (openness) wanted, given that it can only be self-reported? The alternative is to drop it and keep curiosity to CU1 and CU2.
3. **Critical thinking:** should CT3 include ethical reasoning or perspective-taking, or is that outside this framework?
4. **Creativity:** is problem finding (CR4) wanted, or should creativity stay limited to idea generation (CR1–CR3)?
5. **Communication:** is written communication alone acceptable for the first version, with spoken communication explicitly deferred?
6. **All skills:** do any of these definitions conflict with how the 5Cs are taught or described to students at Hult?
